## The weekly learning cycle

Each topic is met three times before it is examined:

1. **Thursday:** I give a few homework problems on next week's content.
2. **Friday to Monday:** try to solve them, and read the book sections they correspond to, so that you come to the lecture with questions.
3. **Tuesday:** I lecture the material;
afterwards you try the problems again.
4. **Wednesday evening:** the teaching assistants' video solution is posted.
**Thursday:** they show how to solve similar problems in MATLAB or Python, and you ask your questions.
5. **The following Tuesday:** an in-class problem or a homework quiz examines the content.

The homework problems handed out each Thursday are the practice problems for the next in-class problem, homework quiz and the final examination.
Learn the worked examples in the lecture notes and the book as well.

## What every quiz and exam covers

Every in-class problem, homework quiz and exam covers everything lectured and every book section in the reading plan up to and including the previous week's lectures.

The final examination covers the whole course.

## Use AI to study

AI such as Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini is always available, answers as many questions as you want, explains in more detail, demonstrates a solution and makes quizzes.
It sometimes hallucinates, giving a wrong answer, so keep asking and questioning what it says.

To restrict it to my lecture notes, use Gemini Notebook, which is free:

1. Open <https://notebooklm.google.com> and sign in with Google.
2. Create a notebook, for example "Automatic Control".
3. Download my lecture PDFs from Moodle and upload them with **Add sources**.
Upload only my lecture notes, not the textbook, which is copyrighted.
4. Ask your question.
Click a numbered citation to see the passage the answer came from, and check it.
5. Ask it to explain a slide step by step, derive an equation, make a quiz, or answer in Traditional Chinese.

Gemini Notebook was called NotebookLM until July 2026.
Help: <https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16215270>

## Practise MATLAB on your own

Free and self-paced:

1. MATLAB Onramp (MathWorks): <https://matlabacademy.mathworks.com/details/matlab-onramp/gettingstarted>
2. Control Design Onramp with Simulink (MathWorks): <https://matlabacademy.mathworks.com/details/control-design-onramp-with-simulink/controls>
3. Appendix C of the course book, Franklin, Powell and Emami-Naeini (2019), lists the MATLAB commands the book uses.

In the course you will also find more MATLAB and Python on the lecture slides, and computer practice led by the teaching assistants on Thursday.
