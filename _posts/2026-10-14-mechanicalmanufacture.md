## Chapter 1: The Structure of Materials

### 2. Describe ionic, covalent, and metallic bonds.
- **題目翻譯**：請分別描述離子鍵、共價鍵與金屬鍵。
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 1` Slide 6, 7
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:
  1. **Ionic Bond**:
     - **Mechanism**: Formed by the complete transfer of one or more valence electrons from an electropositive atom (metal) to an electronegative atom (nonmetal), creating cations and anions held together by strong electrostatic Coulomb attraction (e.g., NaCl).
     - **Properties**: High bond strength, high melting point, and high hardness; but brittle (lacks ductility) and acts as an electrical and thermal insulator at room temperature.
  2. **Covalent Bond**:
     - **Mechanism**: Formed when adjacent atoms share pairs of valence electrons to achieve stable octet electron configurations (e.g., diamond, silicon, and the carbon backbone of polymers).
     - **Properties**: Highly directional, extremely high bond energy, very high hardness and melting point, brittle, and low electrical conductivity.
  3. **Metallic Bond**:
     - **Mechanism**: Valence electrons are delocalized and shared among all positive ion cores, forming a continuous "sea of electrons" that holds the lattice together (e.g., copper, aluminum, iron).
     - **Properties**: Non-directional bonding allows atomic planes to slip without cleavage, imparting high ductility and formability, along with excellent thermal and electrical conductivities.
- **中文觀念解析**：
  - 離子鍵：電子完全轉移形成陰陽離子，依靠靜電引力結合（硬而脆、無自由電子故絕緣）。
  - 共價鍵：相鄰原子共用價電子對，具強方向性（硬度與熔點極高，但呈脆性且導電差）。
  - 金屬鍵：價電子脫離束縛形成自由電子海（非方向性，滑移不易斷鍵故具高延展性，導電導熱優異）。

### 3. What is strain hardening?
- **題目翻譯**：何謂應變硬化（加工硬化）？
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 1` Slide 20; `MFG_chap 2` Slide 12
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:
  - **Definition**: Strain hardening (also called work hardening) is the phenomenon whereby a ductile metal becomes stronger and harder while losing ductility as plastic deformation increases at temperatures below its recrystallization temperature (cold working).
  - **Microscopic Mechanism**: During plastic deformation, the density of dislocations increases dramatically (from $\sim 10^6\text{ cm}^{-2}$ to $10^{11}-10^{12}\text{ cm}^{-2}$). These dislocations move along slip planes, intersect, and become tangled with one another and with grain boundaries. The tangled dislocation networks act as barriers to further dislocation motion, requiring higher external shear stress for continued deformation.
  - **Mathematical Relationship**: Commonly expressed by Hollomon's equation:
    $$\sigma = K \varepsilon^n$$
    where $\sigma$ is true flow stress, $\varepsilon$ is true strain, $K$ is the strength coefficient, and $n$ is the strain-hardening exponent ($0 < n < 1$).
- **中文觀念解析**：
  金屬在常溫冷加工塑性變形時，內部差排大量增生並互相糾結阻礙滑移，宏觀表現為材料的降伏強度與硬度隨塑性應變增加而顯著提高，延展性降低。

### 4. What are grain boundaries?
- **題目翻譯**：何謂晶界（Grain boundaries）？
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 1` Slide 16, 17
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:
  - **Definition**: Grain boundaries are narrow transition interfaces (planar imperfections) that separate individual crystalline grains having different crystallographic orientations, formed when randomly oriented crystals impinge during solidification.
  - **Effects on Mechanical Properties**:
    1. **Barrier to Dislocation Slip**: Because slip planes and directions change abruptly across boundaries, and the boundary atomic arrangement is disordered, dislocations cannot cross freely and pile up at grain boundaries.
    2. **Hall-Petch Strengthening**: Finer grains produce more grain boundary area per unit volume, which increases yield strength and hardness at room temperature according to the Hall-Petch equation:
       $$\sigma_y = \sigma_0 + \frac{k_y}{\sqrt{d}}$$
       where $d$ is the average grain diameter. Grain refinement is the only strengthening mechanism that simultaneously increases strength and toughness.
    3. **Chemical & High-Temperature Behavior**: Grain boundaries have higher energy, making them susceptible to corrosion (intergranular corrosion) and atomic diffusion; at high temperatures, grain boundary sliding can cause creep deformation.
- **中文觀念解析**：
  晶界為不同結晶方位晶粒之間的過渡界面（面缺陷），能有效阻礙差排滑移；晶粒越細小，晶界面積越多，常溫降伏強度與韌性越高（霍爾-佩奇效應）。

### 5. The unit cells shown in the figures can be represented by tennis balls arranged in various configurations in a box. In such an arrangement, the atomic packing factor (APF) is defined as the ratio of the sum of the volumes of the atoms to the volume of the unit cell. Show that the APF is 0.68 for the bcc structure and 0.74 for the fcc structure.
- **題目翻譯**：圖中的單位晶格可以想像成網球排在箱子中。原子堆積因子 (APF) 定義為晶胞內所有原子體積總和與單位晶格體積的比值。請證明 BCC 結構的 APF 為 0.68，FCC 結構的 APF 為 0.74。
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 1` Slide 8, 9, 10
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文完整數學推導)**:

#### (1) Body-Centered Cubic (BCC) Structure:
1. **Lattice Parameter ($a$) and Atomic Radius ($R$)**:
   Atoms touch along the body diagonal:
   $$\sqrt{3} a = 4R \implies a = \frac{4R}{\sqrt{3}}$$
2. **Number of Atoms per Unit Cell ($N_{\text{BCC}}$)**:
   $$N_{\text{BCC}} = 8 \times \left(\frac{1}{8}\right) + 1 = 2\text{ atoms}$$
3. **Volumes**:
   - Volume of atoms:
     $$V_{\text{atoms}} = 2 \times \left(\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3\right) = \frac{8}{3}\pi R^3$$
   - Volume of unit cell:
     $$V_{\text{cell}} = a^3 = \left(\frac{4R}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^3 = \frac{64 R^3}{3\sqrt{3}}$$
4. **Atomic Packing Factor (APF)**:
   $$\text{APF}_{\text{BCC}} = \frac{V_{\text{atoms}}}{V_{\text{cell}}} = \frac{\frac{8}{3}\pi R^3}{\frac{64 R^3}{3\sqrt{3}}} = \frac{\pi \sqrt{3}}{8} \approx \frac{3.14159 \times 1.73205}{8} \approx \mathbf{0.6802} \approx \mathbf{0.68} \quad (68\%)$$

#### (2) Face-Centered Cubic (FCC) Structure:
1. **Lattice Parameter ($a$) and Atomic Radius ($R$)**:
   Atoms touch along the face diagonal:
   $$\sqrt{2} a = 4R \implies a = \frac{4R}{\sqrt{2}} = 2\sqrt{2}R$$
2. **Number of Atoms per Unit Cell ($N_{\text{FCC}}$)**:
   $$N_{\text{FCC}} = 8 \times \left(\frac{1}{8}\right) + 6 \times \left(\frac{1}{2}\right) = 1 + 3 = 4\text{ atoms}$$
3. **Volumes**:
   - Volume of atoms:
     $$V_{\text{atoms}} = 4 \times \left(\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3\right) = \frac{16}{3}\pi R^3$$
   - Volume of unit cell:
     $$V_{\text{cell}} = a^3 = (2\sqrt{2}R)^3 = 16\sqrt{2} R^3$$
4. **Atomic Packing Factor (APF)**:
   $$\text{APF}_{\text{FCC}} = \frac{V_{\text{atoms}}}{V_{\text{cell}}} = \frac{\frac{16}{3}\pi R^3}{16\sqrt{2} R^3} = \frac{\pi}{3\sqrt{2}} = \frac{\pi \sqrt{2}}{6} \approx \frac{3.14159 \times 1.41421}{6} \approx \mathbf{0.7405} \approx \mathbf{0.74} \quad (74\%)$$
*(Note: HCP structure also has an APF of $\frac{\pi}{3\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.74$.)*

## Chapter 2: Mechanical Behavior, Testing, and Manufacturing Properties

### 6. Distinguish between engineering stress and true stress.
- **題目翻譯**：請區別工程應力與真應力。
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 2` Slide 4, 11
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答 - 條列式對比)**:
  #### 1. Engineering Stress (Nominal Stress, $\sigma_e$):
  - **Definition & Formula**: Defined as the applied load divided by the original cross-sectional area:
    $$\sigma_e = \frac{P}{A_0}$$
  - **Cross-Sectional Basis**: Uses fixed initial area $A_0$; does not account for instantaneous reduction in area.
  - **Post-Necking Behavior**: After necking begins, the engineering stress curve falsely bends downward because load drops while $A_0$ is held constant.
  - **Relationship**: Prior to necking, $\sigma = \sigma_e (1 + e)$.
  - **Application**: Used in standard structural design codes, engineering material specifications, and safety factor calculations.

  #### 2. True Stress (Flow Stress, $\sigma$):
  - **Definition & Formula**: Defined as the applied load divided by the actual instantaneous cross-sectional area:
    $$\sigma = \frac{P}{A}$$
  - **Cross-Sectional Basis**: Continuously tracks actual instantaneous area $A$ during deformation.
  - **Post-Necking Behavior**: Continues to rise monotonically until fracture, accurately representing the true resistance of the material to plastic flow.
  - **Application**: Essential for plastic deformation mechanics, metal forming operations (rolling, forging, extrusion), and finite element modeling.
- **中文觀念解析**：
  - 工程應力是以原始橫截面積 $A_0$ 計算，頸縮後載重下降導致曲線虛假下滑。
  - 真應力是以瞬時實際橫截面積 $A$ 計算，真實反映材料當下的流動應力，單調遞增直到斷裂。

### 7. Describe the difference between brittle and ductile fracture.
- **題目翻譯**：請描述脆性破壞與延性破壞之間的差異。
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 2` Slide 30, 31, 33, 34
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答 - 條列式對比)**:
  #### 1. Ductile Fracture:
  - **Plastic Deformation**: Preceded by extensive plastic deformation and noticeable localized necking before failure.
  - **Energy Absorption & Toughness**: High energy absorption; exhibits high fracture toughness.
  - **Crack Propagation**: Stable crack growth; requires continuous application of increasing stress to propagate.
  - **Fracture Surface Appearance**: Dull, fibrous, and dimpled microvoid pattern; classic "cup-and-cone" macroscopic appearance.
  - **Stress Mechanism**: Governed primarily by maximum shear stress along slip planes.

  #### 2. Brittle Fracture:
  - **Plastic Deformation**: Occurs with negligible or no prior plastic deformation; no necking.
  - **Energy Absorption & Toughness**: Minimal energy absorption; exhibits low fracture toughness.
  - **Crack Propagation**: Unstable and catastrophic; cracks propagate at speeds approaching the speed of sound.
  - **Fracture Surface Appearance**: Flat, bright, and crystalline/granular reflective appearance with cleavage facets.
  - **Stress Mechanism**: Governed primarily by maximum normal tensile stress perpendicular to cleavage planes.
- **中文觀念解析**：
  - 延性破壞有明顯頸縮與塑性變形、吸收大量能量（高韌性）、杯錐狀纖維斷口，由剪應力主導。
  - 脆性破壞無塑性變形、吸收能量極少、裂紋失穩超高速擴展、斷口平整明亮具結晶反光，由正拉應力主導。

### 8. What is residual stress? How can residual stresses be removed?
- **題目翻譯**：何謂殘留應力？如何消除殘留應力？
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 2` Slide 36, 37, 38; `MFG_chap 1` Slide 23
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:
  - **Definition**: Residual stresses are internal stresses that remain locked inside a component after all external loads, forces, and thermal gradients have been completely removed. They arise from non-uniform plastic deformation, uneven thermal cooling (in casting/welding), or phase transformations.
  - **Consequences**: Surface tensile residual stresses reduce fatigue life, promote stress-corrosion cracking (SCC), and cause dimensional instability or warping when material is removed during machining.
  - **Methods to Remove Residual Stresses**:
    1. **Thermal Method — Stress-Relief Annealing**: Heating the part to a moderate temperature below recrystallization (e.g., $550^\circ\text{C}-650^\circ\text{C}$ for steels), holding to allow stress relaxation via creep and dislocation rearrangement, followed by very slow furnace cooling.
    2. **Mechanical Methods**:
       - *Stretching / Roller Leveling*: Applying a small, uniform plastic strain (1%–3%) to redistribute and equalize internal stresses.
       - *Shot Peening*: Bombarding the surface with hard media to induce beneficial **compressive residual stresses** that counteract harmful tensile stresses and enhance fatigue resistance.
    3. **Vibratory Stress Relief (VSR)**: Applying cyclic mechanical vibrations at resonance frequencies to accelerate localized dislocation relaxation.
- **中文觀念解析**：
  殘留應力是工件在無任何外力與溫差下內部自我平衡的應力。消除方式包含：應力消除退火（加熱保溫緩冷鬆弛）、機械拉伸整平，或利用珠擊在表層引入有益的殘留壓應力。

### 9. What is the difference between engineering strain and true strain? (5%)
- **題目翻譯**：工程應變與真應變有何差異？
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 2` Slide 4, 11
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:
  1. **Definition & Reference Length**:
     - *Engineering Strain ($e$)*: Defined as $e = \frac{l - l_0}{l_0} = \frac{\Delta l}{l_0}$. Normalized with respect to the **fixed original gage length** ($l_0$).
     - *True Strain ($\varepsilon$)*: Defined as $\varepsilon = \int_{l_0}^l \frac{dl}{l} = \ln\left(\frac{l}{l_0}\right) = \ln(1 + e)$. Integrates incremental deformations normalized with respect to the **instantaneous actual length** ($l$).
  2. **Additivity**: True strain is strictly additive across multistep deformations ($\varepsilon_{\text{total}} = \varepsilon_1 + \varepsilon_2$), whereas engineering strain is not.
  3. **Small vs. Large Deformations**: In the linear elastic region ($e \ll 1$), $\varepsilon \approx e$; under large plastic deformations, true strain accurately reflects the physical state of strain.
- **中文觀念解析**：
  工程應變以固定初始標距 $l_0$ 為分母；真應變以瞬時長度 $l$ 為分母進行微元累加積分（$\varepsilon = \ln(1+e)$），具備數學可加性，為大塑性成型計算的標準。

### 10. Explain why the difference between engineering strain and true strain becomes larger as strain increases. Does this difference occur for both tensile and compressive strains? Explain.
- **題目翻譯**：解釋為何工程應變與真應變的差異會隨著應變增加而變大？拉伸與壓縮應變皆會發生此現象嗎？請說明原因。
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 2` Slide 4, 11, 17
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:
  1. **Why the Difference Grows Larger**:
     - Using the Taylor series expansion of $\varepsilon = \ln(1 + e)$ around $e = 0$:
       $$\varepsilon = e - \frac{e^2}{2} + \frac{e^3}{3} - \frac{e^4}{4} + \dots$$
       $$|e - \varepsilon| \approx \frac{e^2}{2}$$
     - When strain is small ($e \to 0$), higher-order terms are negligible and $\varepsilon \approx e$. As strain increases, the higher-order terms ($e^2/2$, etc.) grow exponentially, widening the gap.
     - *Physical reason*: The engineering strain denominator stays fixed at $l_0$, while the true strain denominator is instantaneous length $l$. As deformation accumulates, $l$ diverges further from $l_0$, creating an increasing discrepancy between $dl/l$ and $dl/l_0$.
  2. **Does It Occur for Both Tensile and Compressive Strains?**
     - **Yes, it occurs for both tensile and compressive strains.**
     - *In Tension ($e > 0$, $l > l_0$)*: Because instantaneous length $l > l_0$, incremental additions $\frac{dl}{l} < \frac{dl}{l_0}$, yielding $\varepsilon = \ln(1+e) < e$. The gap between $e$ and $\varepsilon$ widens with increasing elongation.
     - *In Compression ($e < 0$, $l < l_0$)*: Let nominal compressive strain be $e_c = -\Delta l / l_0 > 0$. Then $|\varepsilon| = |\ln(1 - e_c)| = e_c + \frac{e_c^2}{2} + \frac{e_c^3}{3} + \dots > e_c$. As the part is flattened, $l \ll l_0$, making increments $|dl|/l \gg |dl|/l_0$. Consequently, true compressive strain grows much faster than engineering compressive strain (e.g., at 80% compression, $e_c = 0.8$, whereas $|\varepsilon| = \ln 5 \approx 1.609$).
- **中文觀念解析**：
  泰勒展開式二階項 $e^2/2$ 隨變形增大迅速放大。拉伸與壓縮皆發生：拉伸時 $\varepsilon < e$；壓縮時絕對值 $|\varepsilon| > e_c$，兩者差距皆隨應變增大而急遽拉開。

### 11. Draw a typical stress-strain curve. Point out where is ultimate tensile strength and where is yield stress.
- **題目翻譯**：繪製典型的應力-應變曲線，並標記極限抗拉強度 (UTS) 與降伏應力 (Yield Stress) 的位置。
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 2` Slide 4, 5, 6, 7, 8
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:

```text
 Engineering Stress (σ)
   ^
   |                                UTS (Ultimate Tensile Strength)
   |                                 * (Peak stress / Onset of Necking)
   |                               /   \
   |                              /     \
   |                             /       \   * Fracture Point
   |           Yield Stress (Y) /         \
   |           (0.2% Offset)   /           \ (Localized Necking Region)
   |                  *-------/             \
   |                 / (Strain Hardening Region)
   |                /
   |               / (Uniform Plastic Deformation)
   |              /
   |             * Proportional Limit
   |            /
   |           /  Slope = Young's Modulus (E)
   |          /
   |         / (Linear Elastic Region)
   |        /
   +-------+--------------------------------------------->
   0     0.2%                                       Engineering Strain (e)
         Offset (0.002)
```

- **Key Landmark Definitions**:
  1. **Yield Stress / Yield Strength ($Y$ or $\sigma_y$)**: The stress level dividing linear elastic behavior from permanent plastic deformation. In standard engineering testing, it is determined by drawing a line parallel to the elastic slope ($E$) starting at **0.2% (0.002) offset strain**; the intersection point with the stress-strain curve is the **0.2% offset yield stress**.
  2. **Ultimate Tensile Strength (UTS or $\sigma_u$)**: The **maximum peak stress** on the engineering stress-strain curve. It represents the highest nominal load a specimen carries and marks the transition from uniform plastic deformation to the **onset of localized necking**.

### 12. Will the disk test be applicable to a ductile material? Why or why not?
- **題目翻譯**：圓盤試驗（Disk test）適用於延性材料嗎？為什麼？
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 2` Slide 18; `MFG_chap 8`
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:
  - **Answer: No, the disk test is NOT applicable to ductile materials.**
  - **Reason**:
    1. *Original Purpose & Theory*: The disk test (Brazilian test) is designed specifically for **brittle materials** (ceramics, concrete, rock, powder compacts) whose tensile strength is much lower than their compressive strength. Elastic theory demonstrates that compressive line-loading across the diameter generates a uniform transverse tensile stress:
       $$\sigma_t = \frac{2P}{\pi d t}$$
       Brittle materials split along the vertical diameter with negligible deformation, allowing direct calculation of tensile fracture strength.
    2. *Behavior of Ductile Materials*: When a ductile material is tested, it undergoes extensive **plastic deformation, contact flattening, and barreling** under compressive loading without splitting vertically. This severe plastic flow completely invalidates the elastic boundary assumptions of the transverse tensile stress formula, making it impossible to determine tensile strength.
- **中文觀念解析**：
  圓盤試驗專為脆性材料劈裂設計。延性材料受壓只會被壓扁鼓肚塑性變形，不會沿直徑脆性劈裂，彈性力學公式完全失效。

### 13. The design specification for a metal requires a minimum hardness of 60 HRA. If a Rockwell test is performed and the depth of penetration is 65 μm, is the material acceptable?

## Chapter 3: Physical Properties of Materials

### 15. What is the difference between thermal conductivity and thermal expansion?
- **題目翻譯**：熱傳導率（Thermal conductivity）與熱膨脹（Thermal expansion）有何差異？
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 3` Slide 7, 8
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答 - 條列式對比)**:
  #### 1. Thermal Conductivity ($k$):
  - **Physical Definition**: Measures the **rate at which thermal energy (heat) flows within and through a material** under an applied temperature gradient.
  - **Governing Formula**: Fourier's Law of Conduction:
    $$q = -k \frac{dT}{dx}$$
  - **Standard Units**: $\text{W}/(\text{m}\cdot\text{K})$ or $\text{Btu}/(\text{hr}\cdot\text{ft}\cdot^\circ\text{F})$.
  - **Microscopic Mechanism**: Heat is transported primarily by **free electron migration** in metals and by **lattice phonon vibrations** in nonmetals. Metallic-bonded materials possess the highest $k$.
  - **Manufacturing Significance**: Critical for heat dissipation in cutting tools, radiator cooling fins, casting mold chilling rates, and controlling heat-affected zones (HAZ) during welding.

  #### 2. Thermal Expansion ($\alpha$):
  - **Physical Definition**: Measures the **relative rate of dimensional change (expansion or contraction)** in a material per unit change in temperature.
  - **Governing Formula**: Linear Thermal Strain Relationship:
    $$\frac{\Delta L}{L_0} = \alpha \Delta T$$
  - **Standard Units**: $1/\text{K}$, $1/^\circ\text{C}$, or $\mu\text{m}/(\text{m}\cdot^\circ\text{C})$.
  - **Microscopic Mechanism**: Increasing thermal energy increases atomic vibrational amplitude; asymmetry in the interatomic potential energy well leads to an **increased mean equilibrium interatomic separation**.
  - **Manufacturing Significance**: Dictates shrinkage allowances in casting pattern design, thermal expansion clearances in tooling, thermal stresses, and resistance to thermal fatigue cracking.
- **中文觀念解析**：
  - 熱傳導率 $k$ 代表材料傳導熱量的速率快慢（傅立葉定律），金屬靠自由電子傳導效率最高。
  - 熱膨脹係數 $\alpha$ 代表材料隨溫度升降產生尺寸伸縮的程度，影響鑄造收縮補償與熱應力裂紋。

### 16. What is the fundamental difference between mechanical properties of materials and physical properties of materials?
- **題目翻譯**：材料的「機械性質」與「物理性質」之間有何根本上的差異？
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 0` Slide 7; `MFG_chap 2` Slide 1; `MFG_chap 3` Slide 1, 2
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:
  1. **Mechanical Properties**:
     - **Fundamental Definition**: Describe how a material responds to **applied external mechanical forces, loads, or stresses** through deformation, yielding, hardening, or fracture.
     - **Nature**: Inherently **stress-dependent and deformation-driven**, typically measured by destructive testing (e.g., tensile, hardness, impact tests), and strongly influenced by processing history and microstructure.
     - **Examples**: Yield strength, ultimate tensile strength (UTS), hardness, ductility, Young's modulus, toughness, fatigue limit, and creep strength.
  2. **Physical Properties**:
     - **Fundamental Definition**: Describe the **intrinsic, constitutive characteristics** of a material governed by its atomic number, electronic configuration, and chemical composition in the **absence of external mechanical loads**.
     - **Nature**: Non-destructive responses to thermal, electrical, magnetic, and optical fields that do not induce macro-mechanical deformation.
     - **Examples**: Density, melting point, thermal conductivity, specific heat, coefficient of thermal expansion, electrical resistivity, and magnetic permeability.
- **中文觀念解析**：
  機械性質是材料受「外加機械力」時的變形與破壞響應（破壞性試驗，如強度、硬度、延性）；物理性質是無外力作用下的材料固有特徵（如密度、熔點、導熱率、熱膨脹係數）。

### 18. Describe the characteristics of (a) an alloy, (b) pearlite, (c) austenite, (d) martensite, and (e) cementite.
- **題目翻譯**：請分別描述下列微觀組織與相的特性：(a) 合金、(b) 波來鐵、(c) 沃斯田鐵、(d) 麻田散鐵、(e) 雪明碳鐵。

- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:
  1. **(a) An Alloy**:
     - A metallic substance composed of two or more chemical elements, at least one of which is a metal.
     - Can exist as a **solid solution** (substitutional or interstitial) or a **two-phase system**. Alloying impedes dislocation motion, providing substantially higher strength, hardness, and corrosion resistance compared to pure metals.
  2. **(b) Pearlite**:
     - A two-phase lamellar microstructure consisting of **alternating parallel plates of ferrite ($\alpha$) and cementite ($\text{Fe}_3\text{C}$)**.
     - Formed by the eutectoid decomposition of austenite at $727^\circ\text{C}$ (0.77% C). Combines the ductility of ferrite with the hardness of cementite, exhibiting moderate strength, ductility, and good machinability.
  3. **(c) Austenite ($\gamma$ phase)**:
     - The **face-centered cubic (FCC)** solid solution of carbon in iron, stable in pure iron between $912^\circ\text{C}$ and $1394^\circ\text{C}$.
     - Possesses a high maximum carbon solubility of **2.11 wt% C at $1148^\circ\text{C}$**.
     - Characterized by being **non-magnetic (paramagnetic)**, highly ductile, tough, and easily formable at elevated temperatures; forms the parent phase for hardening heat treatments.
  4. **(d) Martensite**:
     - A supersaturated solid solution of carbon in **body-centered tetragonal (BCT)** iron, formed by a diffusionless, athermal shear transformation when austenite is rapidly quenched below the $M_s$ temperature.
     - Features high internal dislocation density and severe lattice distortion.
     - Characterized by **extreme hardness, high yield strength, severe brittleness**, and high internal quenching residual stresses; must be tempered before service.
  5. **(e) Cementite ($\text{Fe}_3\text{C}$ / Iron Carbide)**:
     - An intermetallic compound containing a fixed stoichiometry of **6.67 wt% C** with an orthorhombic crystal structure.
     - Characterized by being **extremely hard (> 65 HRC) and wear-resistant, but extremely brittle** with virtually zero tensile ductility. Acts as the primary strengthening phase in carbon steels.
- **中文觀念解析**：
  - 合金：由金屬與其他元素組成，具金屬特性的均勻固體。
  - 波來鐵：肥粒鐵與雪明碳鐵之層狀共析組織（兼具強度與延性）。
  - 沃斯田鐵：高溫 FCC 相，溶碳量高達 2.11%，無磁性、延韌性佳。
  - 麻田散鐵：急冷無擴散相變生成的過飽和 BCT 相，極硬且脆。
  - 雪明碳鐵：碳化三鐵化合物（6.67% C），硬度極高耐磨但極脆。

### 20. Explain the differences of annealing, tempering, and quenching.
- **題目翻譯**：請詳細說明退火（Annealing）、回火（Tempering）與淬火（Quenching）的差異。
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 4` Slide 30, 34, 35, 41, 45, 49
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答 - 分項結構化對比)**:
  #### 1. Annealing (退火):
  - **Heating Temperature**: Heated above critical temperatures ($A_3$ or $A_1$) into the austenite region, followed by extended soaking.
  - **Cooling Method & Rate**: **Very slow cooling inside the furnace (Furnace Cooling)**.
  - **Microstructure Formed**: Coarse equilibrium structure of **ferrite + coarse pearlite / spheroidite**.
  - **Purpose & Result**: Softens the steel, **reduces hardness and strength, maximizes ductility and toughness**, relieves internal residual stresses, and refines grain structure for subsequent cold forming or machining.

  #### 2. Quenching (淬火):
  - **Heating Temperature**: Fully austenitized above $A_3$ or $A_1$ ($30^\circ\text{C}-50^\circ\text{C}$ above).
  - **Cooling Method & Rate**: **Rapid cooling / severe chill** by immersing into agitated brine, water, or oil.
  - **Microstructure Formed**: Diffusionless shear transformation produces **BCT Martensite**.
  - **Purpose & Result**: **Maximizes hardness and wear resistance**; severely embrittles the steel and induces high internal residual stresses (cannot be put into engineering service without tempering).

  #### 3. Tempering (回火):
  - **Heating Temperature**: Reheated **below the lower critical temperature $A_1$** ($150^\circ\text{C}-650^\circ\text{C}$) after quenching.
  - **Cooling Method & Rate**: Cooled in still air (or oil).
  - **Microstructure Formed**: Martensite decomposes into **tempered martensite** (extremely fine carbide particles dispersed in a ferrite matrix).
  - **Purpose & Result**: Relieves severe quenching stresses, reduces excessive brittleness, and **significantly restores impact toughness and ductility** while retaining high tensile strength.
- **中文觀念解析**：
  - 退火：高溫保溫後爐冷（軟化、提高延展性、消除應力）。
  - 淬火：沃斯田鐵化後急冷水淬/油淬（生成麻田散鐵，硬度極高但脆性大）。
  - 回火：淬火後在臨界點以下二次加熱空冷（去淬火內應力，大幅提升衝擊韌性）。

### 22. What is the difference between carbon steel and stainless steel? Why is the stainless steel stainless?
- **題目翻譯**：碳鋼與不鏽鋼有何不同？為什麼不鏽鋼能夠「不生鏽」？
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 5` Slide 2, 16, 22
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:
  1. **Difference between Carbon Steel and Stainless Steel**:
     - *Carbon Steel*: An iron-carbon alloy containing small amounts of manganese and silicon without significant corrosion-resistant alloying additions. In moist air or water, iron oxidizes into loose, porous, flaky iron oxide (rust, $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3\cdot n\text{H}_2\text{O}$), allowing continuous oxygen diffusion and progressive corrosion.
     - *Stainless Steel*: An alloy steel containing a **substantial amount of chromium—at least 10.5% to 12% Cr by weight** (frequently 12%–30% Cr, often combined with nickel, molybdenum). It offers superior corrosion resistance, high-temperature oxidation resistance, and aesthetic appeal.
  2. **Why Stainless Steel is "Stainless"**:
     - *Passive Protective Film Formation*: When exposed to oxygen (in air or water), chromium in the steel reacts preferentially to form an **ultra-thin (nanometer-scale), dense, coherent, and chemically stable chromium oxide ($\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_3$) passive film** on the surface.
     - *Self-Healing Capacity*: This impervious passive layer blocks oxygen and moisture from attacking the underlying substrate. If the surface is scratched or mechanically damaged, **the chromium oxide film immediately re-forms (re-passivates) spontaneously in the presence of oxygen**, providing permanent rust protection.
- **中文觀念解析**：

### 24. What is the definition of Ultra-high-strength Steels?
- **題目翻譯**：超高強度鋼（Ultra-high-strength Steels, UHSS）的定義為何？
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 5` Slide 20
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:

     - *High Raw Material Extraction Cost*: Refining titanium from rutile/ilmenite via the multi-step Kroll Process requires high-energy vacuum distillation and magnesium reduction in inert gas, resulting in raw material costs 10–30 times higher than steel or aluminum.

       - Low thermal conductivity concentrates cutting heat at the tool tip, causing rapid tool wear.
       - Low elastic modulus causes workpiece chatter and tool deflection.
       - High chemical reactivity at elevated temperatures requires expensive vacuum or inert gas shielding during welding and casting.
     - *Cost Sensitivity of Consumer Vehicles*: Mass-market passenger cars prioritize manufacturing economics; conventional cast iron and aluminum alloys provide sufficient performance at a fraction of the cost.

### 26. What factors other than mechanical strength should be considered in selecting metals and alloys for high-temperature applications? Explain.
- **題目翻譯**：在選擇用於高溫環境的金屬與合金時，除了常溫機械強度外，還應考慮哪些重要因素？請詳細解釋。

## Chapter 7: Polymers

### 28. Explain the differences between linear, branched, and cross-linked polymers.
- **題目翻譯**：請詳細解釋線狀（Linear）、分枝狀（Branched）與交聯狀（Cross-linked）高分子聚合物之間的結構與性質差異。
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 7` Slide 10, 11, 12, 13
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答 - 分項結構化對比)**:
  #### 1. Linear Polymers:
  - **Molecular Architecture**: Monomer units form long, continuous, sequential chainlike backbones (like cooked spaghetti) with no major side branches.
  - **Intermolecular Bonding**: Covalent bonds along the main chain; **adjacent chains are held together exclusively by weak secondary bonds (van der Waals or hydrogen bonds)**.
  - **Characteristics**: Allows high chain packing density; melts and flows reversibly upon heating as weak secondary bonds soften; classic thermoplastic behavior.
  - **Examples**: High-Density Polyethylene (HDPE), Polyvinyl Chloride (PVC), Polymethyl Methacrylate (PMMA), Nylon.

  #### 2. Branched Polymers:
  - **Molecular Architecture**: Side-branch chains originate from and are attached to the main polymer backbone during polymerization.
  - **Intermolecular Bonding**: Adjacent chains are held by weak secondary bonds, but side branches **increase steric hindrance and prevent tight, orderly chain packing**.
  - **Characteristics**: Reduced degree of crystallinity, lower density, lower tensile stiffness and strength, and lower melting temperature than linear polymers; good flowability during molding.
  - **Example**: Low-Density Polyethylene (LDPE).

  #### 3. Cross-linked Polymers:
  - **Molecular Architecture**: Adjacent polymer chains are interconnected by **strong primary covalent chemical bonds into a three-dimensional network structure**.
  - **Intermolecular Bonding**: Chains are **permanently anchored by covalent cross-links**; individual chains cannot slide past one another.
  - **Characteristics**: Behaves as a single giant macromolecule; will not soften or melt upon heating (irreversibly degrades/chars at high temperature); exhibits high hardness, stiffness, dimensional stability, and creep resistance.
  - **Examples**: Epoxy, Phenolics (Bakelite), Vulcanized Rubber.
- **中文觀念解析**：
  - 線狀：長條主鏈，鏈間靠弱二次鍵結合，加熱易軟化熔融，具熱塑性。
  - 分枝狀：有側鏈阻礙緊密堆疊，結晶度與剛性較低。
  - 交聯狀：鏈間以強共價鍵形成三維網狀結構，加熱不熔化只會焦化，高剛性高耐溫。

### 29. What is glass-transition temperature?
- **題目翻譯**：何謂玻璃轉移溫度（Glass-transition temperature, $T_g$）？
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 7` Slide 19, 20
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:
  - **Definition**: The **glass-transition temperature ($T_g$)** is the critical temperature at which an amorphous polymer (or the amorphous regions in a semicrystalline polymer) undergoes a reversible transition from a **hard, rigid, brittle, and glassy state** at lower temperatures to a **soft, flexible, rubbery, or leathery state** at higher temperatures.
  - **Molecular Mechanism**:
    - *Below $T_g$ ($T < T_g$)*: Thermal kinetic energy is insufficient to overcome energy barriers to bond rotation. Polymer chain segments are "frozen" in place, resulting in high elastic modulus, high stiffness, and brittle behavior.
    - *Above $T_g$ ($T > T_g$)*: Thermal energy activates coordinated rotations and sliding of long-chain segments. The elastic modulus drops by several orders of magnitude, and ductility and impact toughness increase dramatically.
  - **Engineering Importance**:
    - Structural engineering plastics (e.g., PMMA, PC, Polystyrene) must have $T_g$ **well above ambient operating temperature** to maintain rigidity and structural strength.
    - Elastomers and rubbers must have $T_g$ **well below ambient operating temperature** (e.g., natural rubber $T_g \approx -70^\circ\text{C}$) to maintain flexibility and elastic resilience.
- **中文觀念解析**：
  $T_g$ 是非晶質聚合物由低溫硬脆玻璃態轉變為高溫柔軟橡膠態的臨界溫度。結構塑料常溫需低於 $T_g$ 以保證剛性；橡膠彈性體常溫需高於 $T_g$ 以保證彈性。

### 30. What is the difference between thermoplastics and thermosetting plastics?
- **題目翻譯**：熱塑性塑膠（Thermoplastics）與熱固性塑膠（Thermosets）有何本質上的差異？
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 7` Slide 22, 23, 31, 32
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答 - 分項結構化對比)**:
  #### 1. Molecular Structure & Bonding:
  - **Thermoplastics**: Consist of **linear or branched** long chains. Covalent bonds form the backbone, while **weak secondary bonds (van der Waals)** hold adjacent chains together.
  - **Thermosets**: Consist of a **three-dimensional cross-linked network** formed by strong primary covalent bonds throughout, forming a single giant macromolecule.

  #### 2. Thermal Response & Reversibility:
  - **Thermoplastics**: **Soften and melt when heated, and solidify when cooled. The process is fully REVERSIBLE**, allowing repeated remelting and reshaping.
  - **Thermosets**: Curing (cross-linking) is **IRREVERSIBLE**. They do not melt upon reheating; excessive temperature leads to chemical degradation, charring, and thermal decomposition.

  #### 3. Manufacturing Processes:
  - **Thermoplastics**: Formed by injection molding, extrusion, blow molding, and thermoforming.
  - **Thermosets**: Formed by compression molding, transfer molding, and reaction injection molding (RIM).

  #### 4. Mechanical & Environmental Properties:
  - **Thermoplastics**: Higher ductility, greater impact toughness, but lower creep resistance and lower maximum operating temperatures; soluble in specific organic solvents.
  - **Thermosets**: High hardness, high stiffness, exceptional dimensional stability, **outstanding resistance to creep and chemical solvents**.

  #### 5. Representative Examples:
  - **Thermoplastics**: Polyethylene (PE), Polypropylene (PP), Polyvinyl Chloride (PVC), Polystyrene (PS), ABS, Polycarbonate (PC), Nylon.
  - **Thermosets**: Epoxy, Phenolic (Bakelite), Polyester, Melamine.
- **中文觀念解析**：
  - 熱塑性塑膠：線狀/分枝狀，靠弱二次鍵結合，加熱熔化冷卻固化過程完全可逆，易於回收。
  - 熱固性塑膠：強共價鍵三維交聯網絡，固化反應不可逆，加熱不熔化只會焦解碳化。

### 31. Explain why thermoplastics are easier to recycle than thermosets.
- **題目翻譯**：請詳細解釋為何熱塑性塑膠比熱固性塑膠更容易回收再生利用？
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 7` Slide 22, 31, 40
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:
  1. **Recyclability of Thermoplastics**:
     - In thermoplastics, individual polymer chains are held together exclusively by **weak secondary bonds (van der Waals or hydrogen bonds)**.
     - When collected and heated above their glass-transition temperature ($T_g$) or melting temperature ($T_m$), thermal energy breaks these secondary bonds without breaking the covalent backbone.
     - The polymer melts and flows into a viscous liquid, allowing it to be filtered, pelletized, and **re-injected or extruded into new products repeatedly**. Cooling re-establishes the secondary bonds, making mechanical recycling highly economical and straightforward.
  2. **Non-Recyclability of Thermosets**:
     - Thermosets undergo irreversible chemical cross-linking during curing, creating a continuous **three-dimensional network of strong primary covalent bonds**.
     - Because covalent bond energy is extremely high, heating **cannot induce melting or fluid flow**.
     - If heated to excessive temperatures, the covalent bonds break catastrophically, causing irreversible **thermal decomposition, charring, and hazardous outgassing**.
     - Thermosets cannot be remelted and remolded; they can only be mechanically ground into coarse filler particles or chemically degraded at high cost, making conventional recycling commercially impractical.
- **中文觀念解析**：
  熱塑性分子鏈間為弱二次鍵，加熱可逆熔融再成形；熱固性鏈間為強共價交聯網絡，無法加熱融化，高溫直接焦化分解，無法簡易熔融再生。

### 32. What is elastomer?
- **題目翻譯**：何謂彈性體（Elastomer / 橡膠 Rubber）？
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 7` Slide 41, 42, 43
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:
  - **Definition**: An **elastomer** is an amorphous polymer exhibiting a very low glass-transition temperature ($T_g$ well below room temperature) and a lightly cross-linked molecular structure. It is capable of undergoing **exceptionally large elastic deformations (several hundred percent strain) without rupture**, and rapidly recovering its original shape and dimensions once the applied load is removed.
  - **Four Key Structural Characteristics**:
    1. *Low Glass-Transition Temperature*: Operating temperatures are well above $T_g$ (e.g., $-70^\circ\text{C}$ for natural rubber), keeping chains in an active, rubbery state.
    2. *Kinked and Coiled Molecular Chains*: In the unstressed state, polymer chains are randomly coiled in a high-entropy state.
    3. *Entropy-Driven Elastic Recovery*: When stretched, coiled chains uncoil and align in the loading direction; upon unloading, thermodynamic entropy forces chains to snap back into coiled conformations immediately.
    4. *Light Cross-linking (Vulcanization)*: Sparse covalent cross-links prevent adjacent chains from permanently slipping past one another, preventing permanent viscous flow.
  - *Examples*: Natural rubber (polyisoprene), styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR), silicone rubber, neoprene.
- **中文觀念解析**：
  彈性體為室溫下處於高彈性橡膠態（$T_g$ 遠低於常溫）且具稀疏交聯點的非晶聚合物，受拉伸可達數倍伸長率，釋放外力後能迅速完全彈性回覆。

### 33. Explain how cross-linking improves the strength of polymers?
- **題目翻譯**：請詳細解釋交聯（Cross-linking）是如何提高高分子聚合物的強度？
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 7` Slide 12, 31; Slide 41
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:
  Cross-linking enhances the strength and stiffness of polymers through the following microscopic mechanisms:
  1. **Replacement of Weak Secondary Bonds with Primary Covalent Bonds**:
     Uncross-linked linear chains are held together only by weak secondary forces (van der Waals or hydrogen bonds, $\sim 2-20\text{ kJ/mol}$). Cross-linking creates strong covalent chemical bonds ($\sim 350-400\text{ kJ/mol}$) directly connecting adjacent chains.
  2. **Physical Restriction of Molecular Chain Slippage**:
     When external tensile or shear loads are applied to an uncross-linked polymer, individual chains easily slide, unravel, and flow past each other. Covalent cross-links physically anchor the chains together, **preventing irreversible chain slippage, uncoiling, and viscous creep**.
  3. **Efficient Stress Redistribution Across the 3D Network**:
     The interconnected network distributes localized mechanical stresses uniformly throughout the surrounding molecular framework via stiff covalent cross-link bridges, increasing the force required to induce deformation.
  - **Resulting Mechanical Properties**:
    - **Marked increase in tensile strength, hardness, and elastic modulus (stiffness)**.
    - **Enhanced resistance to high-temperature creep and solvent swelling**.
    - *(Trade-off: Ductility and elongation at break decrease, making the polymer more brittle)*.
- **中文觀念解析**：
  交聯在相鄰分子鏈間建立高能量的一級共價鍵，在物理上徹底錨定並阻止分子鏈相互滑移，將應力均勻分散至三維網絡中，從而顯著提高硬度、強度與剛性。

## Chapter 8: Ceramics, Graphite, Diamond and Nanomaterials

### 34. Explain why the mechanical properties of ceramics generally differ from those of metals.
- **題目翻譯**：請解釋為何陶瓷材料與金屬材料的機械性質會有根本性的巨大差異？
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 1` Slide 6, 7, 12, 13; `MFG_chap 8` Slide 3, 4, 14, 16
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:
  The profound differences in mechanical properties between ceramics (high hardness, high compressive strength, extreme brittleness) and metals (high ductility, toughness) arise from their fundamental **atomic bonding** and **dislocation mobility**:
  1. **Atomic Bonding Nature**:
     - *Metals (Metallic Bonding)*: Non-directional bonding with a delocalized "sea of electrons". Ions can shift positions relative to one another under shear stress without breaking electrostatic equilibrium.
     - *Ceramics (Ionic & Covalent Bonding)*: Atoms are bound by strong, localized directional covalent bonds or ionic bonds between cations and anions. The binding energy is extremely high.
  2. **Dislocation Mobility and Slip Systems**:
     - *Metals*: Crystal lattices have high symmetry and multiple independent slip systems (e.g., 12 in FCC, 48 in BCC). Dislocations move easily under low critical resolved shear stress, giving metals high ductility and plastic formability.
     - *Ceramics*:
       - In ionic ceramics, shear displacement forces **ions of like charge adjacent to each other**, generating massive electrostatic repulsion that cleaves the crystal.
       - In covalent ceramics, moving dislocations requires breaking strong, highly directional covalent bonds.
       - Consequently, **dislocations are virtually immobile at room temperature**, precluding plastic deformation.
  3. **Sensitivity to Flaws and Griffith's Criterion**:
     - Metals undergo localized plastic yielding around crack tips, blunting the crack and absorbing fracture energy.
     - Ceramics cannot undergo crack-tip plastic blunting. Inherent microflaws, internal pores, and microcracks from powder processing act as severe stress raisers. Under applied tensile stress, local stresses at crack tips easily reach theoretical bond strength, causing **catastrophic brittle cleavage propagation**.
- **中文觀念解析**：
  金屬具非方向性金屬鍵與豐富滑移系統，差排運動自由，具備高延展性與塑性鈍化裂紋能力；陶瓷為方向性共價鍵或強離子鍵，滑移會造成同性電荷排斥，常溫差排被釘死無法滑移，對微裂紋極度敏感，表現為高硬脆斷。

### 36. Explain why ceramic parts may distort or warp during drying. What precautions should be taken to avoid this situation?
- **題目翻譯**：解釋為何陶瓷生胚零件在乾燥過程中容易產生扭曲或翹曲？為避免此情況應採取哪些防範措施？
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 8` & Kalpakjian Processing of Ceramics
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:
  1. **Why Distortion and Warpage Occur During Drying**:
     - *Differential Drying Shrinkage*: Green ceramic compacts contain substantial liquid moisture/binder filling the spaces between powder particles. Moisture must migrate by capillary action from the interior to the external surface to evaporate.
     - *Uneven Shrinkage Stresses*: As moisture evaporates, capillary meniscus forces draw particles closer together, causing overall volumetric shrinkage (often 5%–15%).
     - If drying is rapid or non-uniform, the exterior surface and thinner cross-sections dry and shrink much faster than the moist interior and thick sections. This differential shrinkage sets up severe **surface tensile stresses and internal compressive stresses**, leading to permanent warpage, distortion, and surface cracking.
  2. **Precautions to Avoid Distortion and Warping**:
     - *Precise Climate Control (Controlled Humidity & Temperature)*: Maintain high relative humidity in the early stages of drying to suppress premature surface evaporation, gradually reducing humidity and increasing temperature as internal moisture diffuses outwards.
     - *Uniform Wall Thickness & Generous Radii*: Design ceramic parts with uniform cross-sectional thickness and rounded corners to ensure equal shrinkage rates.
     - *Symmetrical Air Circulation*: Ensure uniform airflow around all surfaces of the part to prevent one-sided rapid drying.

### 37. What is the purpose of firing in ceramic processing?
- **題目翻譯**：在陶瓷加工製程中，「燒成 / 燒結（Firing / Sintering）」的目的為何？
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 8`
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:
  The green ceramic compact after forming and drying consists of weakly bonded powders with low structural strength. **Firing (or sintering) involves heating the green compact in a kiln to an elevated temperature (typically $0.7-0.9 T_m$) below its melting point**. Its primary purposes are:
  1. **Solid-State Diffusion and Interparticle Bonding**: High thermal activation accelerates atomic diffusion across particle contact points, forming and growing neck regions (sintering necks) that bond loose particles with strong chemical bonds.
  2. **Densification and Porosity Reduction**: Driven by the thermodynamic reduction in surface free energy, pore volume shrinks and voids are eliminated, producing a highly dense, coherent polycrystal.
  3. **Development of High Strength and Hardness**: Converts a fragile, powdery compact into a strong, rigid engineering ceramic with exceptional compressive strength, high elastic modulus, and superior wear resistance.
  4. **Phase Transformation and Vitrification**: In clay-based ceramics, firing melts flux components into a viscous liquid phase that flows into remaining pores, solidifying upon cooling into a continuous glassy matrix that seals the structure and prevents permeability.
- **中文觀念解析**：

### 38. Compare the mechanical properties between metals and ceramics.
- **題目翻譯**：請全面比較金屬材料與陶瓷材料之間的機械性質差異。
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 1`; `MFG_chap 2`; `MFG_chap 8` Slide 14, 16
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答 - 分項結構化對比)**:
  #### 1. Hardness and Wear Resistance:
  - **Metals**: Moderate to high (up to 60–65 HRC for quenched tool steels). Prone to abrasive scratching by hard mineral particles.
  - **Ceramics**: **Extremely high** (Mohs hardness 9–10; e.g., $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3, \text{SiC}, \text{cBN}$, Diamond); exceptional resistance to abrasive and erosive wear.

  #### 2. Ductility and Plastic Formability:
  - **Metals**: **High to excellent**; exhibit substantial plastic elongation and area reduction before fracture, enabling cold and hot forming.
  - **Ceramics**: **Virtually zero ductility**; behave in a purely elastic and brittle manner at room temperature.

  #### 3. Tensile versus Compressive Strength:
  - **Metals**: Approximately **symmetric** ($\sigma_{\text{tensile}} \approx \sigma_{\text{compressive}}$).
  - **Ceramics**: **Highly asymmetric**; extremely high compressive strength ($1000-3000+\text{ MPa}$), but low tensile strength (typically only about one-tenth of compressive strength).

  #### 4. Fracture Toughness ($K_{Ic}$):
  - **Metals**: **High** ($K_{Ic} \approx 20-150\text{ MPa}\cdot\text{m}^{1/2}$); able to blunt crack tips via localized plastic yielding.
  - **Ceramics**: **Very low** ($K_{Ic} \approx 1-8\text{ MPa}\cdot\text{m}^{1/2}$); extremely sensitive to surface microcracks, notches, and internal pores.

  #### 5. Modulus of Elasticity (Stiffness):
  - **Metals**: Moderate to high ($\text{Al} \approx 70\text{ GPa}$, Steel $\approx 210\text{ GPa}$, Tungsten $\approx 400\text{ GPa}$).
  - **Ceramics**: **Very high** ($\text{SiC} \approx 400-450\text{ GPa}$, Diamond $\approx 1000\text{ GPa}$); extremely stiff.

  #### 6. Thermal Shock Resistance:
  - **Metals**: High; plastic deformation absorbs thermal stresses.
  - **Ceramics**: Poor; high modulus and lack of plastic flow cause cracking under sudden thermal gradients.

  #### 7. Elevated-Temperature Strength:
  - **Metals**: Degrade significantly above $0.4-0.5 T_m$ due to softening and creep.
  - **Ceramics**: **Outstanding**; retain high strength, hardness, and creep resistance at $1000^\circ\text{C}-1500^\circ\text{C}$.
- **中文觀念解析**：
  金屬具高延展性、高破壞韌性、拉壓對稱；陶瓷硬度極高、抗壓強度遠大於抗拉強度、常溫零延性完全脆性，但高溫強度與抗潛變性能卓越。

### 44. A 100-mm thick square plate and a right circular cylinder with a radius of 100 mm and a height of 25 mm have the same volume. Compare the solidification time of each other.
- **題目翻譯**：一塊厚度為 100 mm 的正方形平板，與一個半徑為 100 mm、高度為 25 mm 的直圓柱體具有相同的體積。請使用徐佛里諾夫法則（Chvorinov's Rule）比較兩者的凝固時間。
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 10` Slide 20
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文推導與計算)**:

#### (1) Fundamental Law: Chvorinov's Rule
Total solidification time $t_s$ is proportional to the square of the volume-to-surface-area ratio (modulus):
$$t_s = C \left(\frac{V}{A}\right)^n$$
For identical mold materials, metals, and pouring temperatures, $C$ and $n$ ($n = 2$) are constants:
$$\frac{t_{s, \text{plate}}}{t_{s, \text{cyl}}} = \left(\frac{(V/A)_{\text{plate}}}{(V/A)_{\text{cyl}}}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{A_{\text{cyl}}}{A_{\text{plate}}}\right)^2 \quad (\because V_{\text{plate}} = V_{\text{cyl}})$$

---

#### (2) Geometric Parameters of the Cylinder:
- Given: Radius $r = 100\text{ mm}$, Height $h = 25\text{ mm}$.
- **Volume ($V_{\text{cyl}}$)**:
  $$V_{\text{cyl}} = \pi r^2 h = \pi (100)^2 (25) = 250,000 \pi \approx 785,398.16\text{ mm}^3$$
- **Total Surface Area ($A_{\text{cyl}}$)** (top, bottom, and side):
  $$A_{\text{cyl}} = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r h = 2\pi(100)^2 + 2\pi(100)(25) = 20,000\pi + 5,000\pi = 25,000\pi \approx 78,539.82\text{ mm}^2$$
- **Modulus of Cylinder ($(V/A)_{\text{cyl}}$)**:
  $$\left(\frac{V}{A}\right)_{\text{cyl}} = \frac{250,000\pi}{25,000\pi} = \mathbf{10.000\text{ mm}}$$

---

#### (3) Geometric Parameters of the Square Plate:
- Thickness $t = 100\text{ mm}$. Let the side length of the square plate be $a$.
- **Volume ($V_{\text{plate}} = V_{\text{cyl}}$)**:
  $$V_{\text{plate}} = a^2 \times t = 100 a^2$$
  Since volumes are identical:
  $$100 a^2 = 250,000 \pi \implies a^2 = 2,500 \pi \approx 7,853.98\text{ mm}^2$$
  $$a = \sqrt{2,500 \pi} = 50 \sqrt{\pi} \approx 50 \times 1.77245385 \approx \mathbf{88.623\text{ mm}}$$
- **Total Surface Area ($A_{\text{plate}}$)** (top, bottom, and 4 sides):
  $$A_{\text{plate}} = 2 a^2 + 4 a t = 2(2,500\pi) + 4(50\sqrt{\pi})(100) = 5,000\pi + 20,000\sqrt{\pi}$$
  $$5,000\pi \approx 15,707.96\text{ mm}^2$$
  $$20,000\sqrt{\pi} \approx 35,449.08\text{ mm}^2$$
  $$A_{\text{plate}} \approx 15,707.96 + 35,449.08 = \mathbf{51,157.04\text{ mm}^2}$$
- **Modulus of Plate ($(V/A)_{\text{plate}}$)**:
  $$\left(\frac{V}{A}\right)_{\text{plate}} = \frac{785,398.16}{51,157.04} \approx \mathbf{15.3527\text{ mm}}$$

---

#### (4) Ratio of Solidification Time:
$$\frac{t_{s, \text{plate}}}{t_{s, \text{cyl}}} = \left(\frac{(V/A)_{\text{plate}}}{(V/A)_{\text{cyl}}}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{15.3527}{10.000}\right)^2 = (1.53527)^2 \approx \mathbf{2.357}$$
Conversely:
$$\frac{t_{s, \text{cyl}}}{t_{s, \text{plate}}} = \frac{1}{2.357} \approx \mathbf{0.424} \quad (42.4\%)$$
- **Conclusion**:
  **The square plate takes approximately 2.36 times longer to solidify than the cylinder (or 136% longer); the cylinder solidifies much faster, taking only ~42.4% of the plate's solidification time.**
- **中文觀念解析**：
  依據徐佛里諾夫定律 $t_s \propto (V/A)^2$，圓柱體之體積表面積比為 10 mm，平板為 15.35 mm。平板因散熱表面積較小，凝固時間約為圓柱體的 2.36 倍。

### 45. Why is heat transfer so important to casting process?

### 46. What is the differences between the solidification of pure metals and metal alloys?
- **題目翻譯**：純金屬與合金在凝固過程中有何本質上的差異？
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 10` Slide 6, 7, 8, 9
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答 - 分項結構化對比)**:
  #### 1. Solidification Temperature:
  - **Pure Metals**: Solidify at a **single, discrete, constant temperature (melting point $T_m$)**, exhibiting a flat isothermal plateau on the cooling curve.
  - **Metal Alloys**: Solidify over a **temperature range** between the liquidus temperature ($T_L$) and solidus temperature ($T_S$); no single freezing point.

  #### 2. Solidification Interface Morphology:
  - **Pure Metals**: Exhibit a sharp, distinct, and well-defined **planar solidification front** advancing progressively inward from the mold walls.
  - **Metal Alloys**: Develop a wide, two-phase **"Mushy Zone"** where liquid and solid dendritic phases coexist simultaneously across the freezing range.

  #### 3. Crystal Growth Morphology:
  - **Pure Metals**: Generate a narrow equiaxed chill zone at the mold wall, followed by smooth **columnar grains growing antiparallel to heat extraction**.
  - **Metal Alloys**: Solidify via complex, highly branched **three-dimensional dendrites** (columnar and equiaxed dendrites) whose arms interlock.

  #### 4. Chemical Segregation & Porosity Defects:
  - **Pure Metals**: Composed of a single chemical element; **no chemical segregation** occurs. Volumetric shrinkage results in a single concentrated piping cavity at the top.
  - **Metal Alloys**: Solute rejection causes severe **microsegregation (coring)** between dendritic arms. Resistance to liquid feeding through narrow interdendritic channels produces widespread **microporosity**.
- **中文觀念解析**：
  - 純金屬在固定熔點恆溫凝固，��清晰平面界面與平直柱狀晶，無偏析。
  - 合金在液相線與固相線之間一段溫區凝固，具有泥狀區（Mushy zone）與三維互鎖樹枝晶，易產生顯微偏析與分散型顯微縮孔。

### 47. What is the purpose of a riser in casting process?
- **題目翻譯**：在金屬鑄造製程中，「冒口 / 冒頭（Riser / Feeder）」的功用為何？
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 10` Slide 12, 20
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:
  - **Primary Purpose**: A **riser (or feeder)** is an auxiliary reservoir of molten metal designed into the mold to **supply additional liquid metal into the casting cavity to compensate for volumetric liquid and solidification shrinkage**, thereby preventing shrinkage cavities (voids) from forming inside the final casting.
  - **Two Critical Design Criteria**:
    1. *Solidification Time Must Exceed Casting Time*: According to Chvorinov's rule, the modulus of the riser must exceed that of the casting section it feeds:
       $$\left(\frac{V}{A}\right)_{\text{riser}} > \left(\frac{V}{A}\right)_{\text{casting}} \implies t_{s, \text{riser}} > t_{s, \text{casting}}$$
       The casting solidifies first, drawing liquid metal from the riser, ensuring shrinkage voids are localized entirely within the disposable riser.
    2. *Adequate Feed Volume & Open Feed Path*: Riser volume must exceed the total shrinkage volume, and the riser neck must not freeze prematurely.
  - **Secondary Functions**: Acts as an air vent for escaping mold gases and collects initial cold, oxidized metal slag during pouring.
- **中文觀念解析**：

### 48. Explain the effects of mold materials on fluid flow and heat transfer in casting operations.
- **題目翻譯**：請詳細說明模具材料對鑄造作業中熔湯的「流體流動」與「熱傳導」之影響。
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 10` Slide 13, 14, 16, 17, 19, 20; `MFG_chap 11` Slide 4, 23
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:
  1. **Effects on Heat Transfer**:
     - *Thermal Conductivity ($k$) and Heat Capacity*:
       - **Metal Permanent Molds (Cast Iron / Tool Steel)**: High thermal conductivity ($k \approx 30-50\text{ W/m}\cdot\text{K}$) rapidly extracts heat, inducing high undercooling, fast solidification, and fine equiaxed grains; but risks premature chilling if filling is slow.
       - **Sand Molds (Silica sand + binders)**: Poor thermal conductivity ($k \approx 0.5-1.5\text{ W/m}\cdot\text{K}$); acts as an insulator, resulting in slow cooling, long solidification times, and coarser grain structures.
       - **Ceramic Molds (Investment shells)**: Can be preheated to $700^\circ\text{C}-1000^\circ\text{C}$ prior to pouring, reducing $\Delta T$ and slowing heat extraction to allow complete filling of ultra-thin, intricate sections.
  2. **Effects on Fluid Flow**:
     - *Surface Roughness and Flow Friction*:
       - **Sand Molds**: Rough, granular cavity walls create high boundary-layer friction, slowing molten metal flow and promoting turbulence; may erode sand into the melt (sand inclusions).
       - **Metal Molds**: Precision CNC-machined and polished surfaces offer low flow friction, smooth laminar filling, and minimal turbulence.
     - *Permeability and Backpressure*:
       - **Sand Molds**: High permeability allows air, steam, and volatile binder gases to vent freely through sand pores, preventing backpressure.
       - **Metal Molds**: Impermeable to gases; requires precisely machined parting-line vents or vacuum evacuation systems to prevent trapped gas pockets and severe backpressure.
- **中文觀念解析**：
  金屬模導熱極快（激冷細晶但易澆不足）、表面光滑阻力小、不透氣需精密排氣；砂模導熱慢（保溫性佳、晶粒粗）、表面粗糙流阻大、透氣性佳。

### 49. Describe the drawbacks to having a riser that is (a) too large and (b) too small.
- **題目翻譯**：請分別描述冒口尺寸 (a) 過大 與 (b) 過小 所帶來的嚴重缺點與不良後果。
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 10` Slide 12, 20
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:
  1. **(a) Drawbacks of a Riser That is Too Large**:
     - *Low Casting Yield*: Wastes significant molten metal, reducing casting yield ($\text{cast weight} / \text{poured weight}$), requiring extra furnace capacity, energy, and remelting costs.
     - *High Removal and Machining Costs*: Cutting, grinding, and machining away a heavy riser neck requires extensive labor, tooling wear, and cycle time.
     - *Extended Cycle Time*: Massive risers take a very long time to solidify and cool, delaying mold breakout and lowering foundry throughput.
     - *Localized Hotspots*: Excess thermal mass at the riser junction can delay local solidification in the casting, causing coarse grains or localized hot tears.
  2. **(b) Drawbacks of a Riser That is Too Small**:
     - *Premature Freezing*: The riser freezes before the casting section it feeds ($t_{s, \text{riser}} < t_{s, \text{casting}}$), violating Chvorinov's rule.
     - *Feeding Failure and Shrinkage Voids*: Liquid metal flow is blocked while the casting core continues to shrink, creating **internal shrinkage cavities, centerline porosity, or surface sink marks**.
     - *Component Scrap and Structural Failure*: Internal voids act as severe stress raisers, destroying pressure tightness and structural integrity, leading to part rejection.
- **中文觀念解析**：
  冒口過大造成金屬良率低、浪費能耗、切除研磨成本高、冷卻週期過長；冒口過小則會提前凍結無法補縮，造成鑄件內部產生致命縮孔與報廢。

### 50. What differences, if any, would you expect in the properties of castings made by permanent-mold versus sandcasting processes?
- **題目翻譯**：相較於砂模鑄造，你預期永久模鑄造所生產的鑄件在各項性質上會有何顯著差異？
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 11` Slide 4, 5, 23
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答 - 分項結構化對比)**:
  #### 1. Grain Structure:
  - **Permanent-Mold Castings**: **Extremely fine grains** due to rapid cooling and intense chilling by high-conductivity metal dies.
  - **Sand Castings**: **Coarser grains** due to slow heat extraction through insulative silica sand.

  #### 2. Mechanical Strength and Ductility:
  - **Permanent-Mold Castings**: **Significantly higher** tensile strength, yield strength, hardness, and fatigue life (driven by Hall-Petch grain refinement).
  - **Sand Castings**: **Lower** strength, hardness, and ductility due to coarse dendritic structures and dispersed microporosity.

  #### 3. Dimensional Precision:
  - **Permanent-Mold Castings**: **High precision** (tight tolerances within $\pm 0.1-0.3\text{ mm}$); high dimensional repeatability.
  - **Sand Castings**: **Lower precision** (tolerances typically $\pm 1.0-3.0\text{ mm}$) due to mold wall movement and pattern drawing variability.

  #### 4. Surface Finish:
  - **Permanent-Mold Castings**: **Smooth finish** ($Ra \approx 1.5-3.5\;\mu\text{m}$); requires minimal or no machining.
  - **Sand Castings**: **Rough finish** ($Ra \approx 10-25\;\mu\text{m}$); shows granular sand texture, requiring larger machining allowances.

  #### 5. Internal Cleanliness and Inclusions:
  - **Permanent-Mold Castings**: Dense and clean; **free of sand inclusions or wash defects**.
  - **Sand Castings**: Prone to **sand wash inclusions and moisture-induced pinhole gas porosity**.

  #### 6. Residual Stresses & Hot Tearing:
  - **Permanent-Mold Castings**: Higher risk of residual stresses and hot tears due to rigid, non-yielding metal mold walls.
  - **Sand Castings**: Sand molds have good **collapsibility**, allowing free contraction and lowering tear risks.
- **中文觀念解析**：
  永久模因金屬導熱快使晶粒極細小，強度與硬度高、尺寸精度高、表面光滑緻密且無夾砂；砂模晶粒粗、強度較低、表面粗糙，但退讓性好且熱裂傾向小。

### 51. Give an example of expandable-mold, expendable-pattern casting processes, and briefly describe this casting process.
- **題目翻譯**：請舉出一種「消耗模、消耗形（Expandable-mold, Expendable-pattern）」的鑄造製程範例，並簡要描述其製程步驟。
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 11` Slide 14, 15, 16, 17
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:
  - **Example**: **Evaporative-Pattern Casting (Lost-Foam Process)** *(or Investment Casting / Lost-Wax Process)*.
  - **Process Description of Lost-Foam Casting**:
    1. *Pattern Molding*: An expendable pattern (including gates and risers) is molded from expanded polystyrene (EPS) beads in a heated aluminum die.
    2. *Refractory Coating*: The assembled foam pattern is dipped in a water-based refractory ceramic slurry and dried, forming a thin, permeable protective ceramic shell.
    3. *Packing in Loose Sand*: The coated pattern is placed in a flask and surrounded by **unbonded, binder-free silica sand**, which is compacted tightly around intricate contours using 3D vibration.
    4. *Pouring and Vaporization*: Molten metal (aluminum or cast iron) is poured directly into the polystyrene sprue. The intense heat of the metal **instantly vaporizes (degrades) the foam pattern**, and molten metal displaces the vapor and completely replicates the cavity.
    5. *Cooling and Shakeout*: After solidification, the sand is dumped out to retrieve the clean casting. The unbonded sand is cooled and 100% recycled.
- **中文觀念解析**：
  代表製程為消失模鑄造法（Lost-foam）。以保麗龍（EPS）發泡成型模型，塗覆耐火漿乾燥後埋入乾砂振動緊實；直接澆注熔湯使保麗龍氣化消失，金屬液取代成型，乾砂倒出取件。

### 52. What is the function of a core?
- **題目翻譯**：在鑄造製程中，「砂心 / 泥心（Core）」的功能為何？
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 11` Slide 9
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:
  - **Definition & Primary Function**: A **core** is a separate, disposable refractory insert (usually made of bonded sand or ceramic) placed into the mold cavity prior to pouring to **form the internal features, cavities, hollow passages, through-holes, or undercuts** of a casting that cannot be produced by the external pattern alone (e.g., automotive engine block water jackets, cylinder bores, hollow valve bodies).
  - **Four Essential Engineering Requirements for Cores**:
    1. *High Hot Strength*: Must resist thermal shock, molten metal erosion, and buoyant upward forces without shifting or breaking.
    2. *Collapsibility*: Must break down and crush easily as the solidifying metal contracts, preventing hot tears and residual cracking in the casting.
    3. *Easy Shakeout*: Must disintegrate easily into loose sand during post-casting cleaning so it can be completely shaken out through small openings.
    4. *High Permeability*: Must vent gases generated by burning organic core binders safely outside the mold, preventing internal gas porosity.
- **中文觀念解析**：
  砂心安放於型腔內部，用以形成鑄件內部中空腔體、水流通道與倒勾；需具備耐沖刷高溫強度、降溫收縮時的退讓性（防熱裂）、透氣性與易敲碎清砂性。

### 53. List the advantages and limitations of die casting.
- **題目翻譯**：請列出壓鑄（Die casting）的優點與其主要局限性。
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 11` Slide 25, 26, 27, 28, 29
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:
  1. **Advantages of Die Casting**:
     - *High Production Rates*: Fast cycle times (200–500 shots per hour for hot-chamber zinc machines; 60–100 shots/hr for cold-chamber aluminum); highly automated.
     - *Excellent Dimensional Accuracy & Precision*: Tight tolerances ($\pm 0.05-0.1\text{ mm}$); produces near-net-shape parts requiring minimal or no machining.
     - *Superior Surface Finish*: Very smooth surfaces ($Ra \approx 0.8-1.6\;\mu\text{m}$) capable of fine surface detailing and ready for plating or painting.
     - *Thin-Walled Complex Geometries*: High injection pressure allows casting ultra-thin walls ($0.5-1.5\text{ mm}$) and intricate structural ribs.
     - *Good Mechanical Strength*: Rapid chill in metal dies produces a dense, fine-grained surface skin.
  2. **Limitations of Die Casting**:
     - *High Capital & Tooling Costs*: Machines and hardened tool-steel dies (e.g., H13) are very expensive, requiring very large production volumes to be economical.
     - *Trapped Gas Porosity*: High injection velocity creates turbulent flow that entraps air and vaporized lubricants, **precluding conventional high-temperature heat treatment (blistering occurs) or welding**.
     - *Part Size & Weight Limits*: Limited by machine clamping tonnage; traditionally limited to small-to-medium castings (< 25 kg).
     - *Restricted to Nonferrous Alloys*: Limited primarily to low-melting nonferrous alloys (zinc, aluminum, magnesium, brass); casting ferrous metals causes severe thermal fatigue and die destruction.
- **中文觀念解析**：
  壓鑄優點為超高生產節拍、高尺寸精度近淨形、表面極光潔、可鑄超薄壁；缺點為模具設備造價極高（需龐大量產）、高速充填易捲氣成氣孔（難以高溫熱處理與焊接）、且限於非鐵低熔點金屬。

### 54. Why are risers not as useful in die casting as they are in sand casting?
- **題目翻譯**：為何冒口（Risers）在壓鑄中不像在砂模鑄造中那麼有用？
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 10` Slide 20; `MFG_chap 11` Slide 25, 27, 28
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:
  Risers are virtually useless in high-pressure die casting for the following physical reasons:
  1. **Direct High-Pressure Feeding by the Hydraulic Plunger**:
     In die casting, molten metal is injected under tremendous pressure ($10-175\text{ MPa}$). Throughout the critical solidification period, the **injection plunger maintains continuous holding pressure**, actively forcing liquid metal into the cavity to feed volumetric shrinkage directly through the runner system. The machine plunger acts as an active, high-pressure feeding system, making passive gravity risers obsolete.
  2. **Rapid Freezing of Thin In-Gates**:
     Die-casting dies are made of high-conductivity steel, and gates connecting the cavity are designed to be very thin ($0.5-2\text{ mm}$). In-gates freeze solid within milliseconds after cavity filling. Any external riser would be cut off immediately by gate freezing and could not deliver molten feed metal.
  3. **Interference with Fast Ejection and Scrap Waste**:
     Die casting relies on high-speed automatic ejection. Risers would complicate multi-slide tooling, prevent automated ejection, waste expensive alloy melt, and drastically increase trimming costs.
- **中文觀念解析**：
  壓鑄依靠液壓射出柱塞施加超高保壓壓力主動補縮（柱塞本身即為超級冒口）；且薄澆口在數毫秒內急冷結凍，外加冒口通道封死無法補縮，還會阻礙自動化脫模與增加成本。

### 55. Explain why squeeze casting produces parts with better mechanical properties, dimensional accuracy, and surface finish than do expendable-mold processes.
- **題目翻譯**：請詳細解釋為何「擠壓鑄造（Squeeze casting）」所生產的鑄件，在機械性質、尺寸精度與表面粗糙度方面，皆顯著優於消耗模（如砂模）製程？
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 11` Slide 33
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:
  Squeeze casting (liquid metal forging) involves pouring molten metal into a preheated metal die and closing an upper punch under **massive hydrostatic pressure ($50-150\text{ MPa}$)** maintained until solidification is complete. It surpasses expendable-mold casting because:
  1. **Superior Mechanical Properties**:
     - *Elimination of Internal Porosity*: The high pressure completely suppresses gas pore nucleation and forces molten metal into interdendritic micro-voids, **eliminating gas porosity and shrinkage cavities completely (100% density)**.
     - *Extremely Fine Equiaxed Grain Structure*: High pressure forces perfect, intimate microscopic contact between molten metal and the high-conductivity tool-steel die walls, eliminating the thermal interfacial air gap. Heat transfer rates increase dramatically, producing extreme undercooling and rapid chilling that yields exceptionally fine, uniform grains with properties rivaling forged components.
  2. **Superior Dimensional Accuracy**:
     - Expendable sand molds suffer from pattern-draw inaccuracies, mold wall movement under liquid metallostatic pressure, and thermal expansion distortions.
     - Squeeze casting uses rigid, high-strength, CNC-machined metal dies closed under rigid hydraulic guidance, eliminating mold flexure and achieving precise tolerances ($\pm 0.05\text{ mm}$).
  3. **Superior Surface Finish**:
     - Sand molds have rough, granular walls that leave granular indentations ($Ra > 10\;\mu\text{m}$).
     - Squeeze casting forces the liquid metal under massive pressure to faithfully imprint against mirror-polished tool-steel die walls, producing a smooth, lustrous finish ($Ra \le 0.8-1.6\;\mu\text{m}$).
- **中文觀念解析**：
  高壓保壓凝固徹底消除縮孔與氣孔達到 100% 緻密；消除模壁氣隙阻抗使導熱急冷暴增，獲得超微細晶粒（媲美鍛造強度）；精密剛性模具保證高尺寸精度與鏡面光潔度。

### 56. Describe the procedures that would be involved in making a large outdoor bronze statue. Which casting process(es) would be suitable? Why?
- **題目翻譯**：請描述製作大型戶外青銅雕像所涉及的具體工藝步驟。哪種（些）鑄造製程最為適合？為什麼？
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 11` Slide 4, 17, 18, 19
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:
  1. **Suitable Casting Process and Justification**:
     - *Suitable Process*: **Segmented Investment Casting (Lost-Wax Process / 脫蠟鑄造法)** or **Resin Sand Casting (樹脂砂模鑄造)**.
     - *Why Suitable*:
       1. *Complex Artistic Detail & Undercuts*: Statues feature intricate organic details and severe undercuts that cannot be drawn from rigid metal permanent dies.
       2. *Low Production Volume (One-off)*: Monumental statues are custom works of art; fabricating permanent metal tooling is cost-prohibitive.
       3. *Hollow Thin-Wall Structure*: Statues must be hollow (wall thickness 5–8 mm) to save tons of costly bronze, prevent foundation collapse, and eliminate severe shrinkage cracking that occurs in thick solid sections.
  2. **Step-by-Step Procedures for Lost-Wax Bronze Casting**:
     - *Step 1: Original Sculpting*: The artist creates a full-scale clay or plaster original statue.
     - *Step 2: Sectioning*: Large sculptures are sectioned into manageable modular pieces (head, torso, limbs).
     - *Step 3: Flexible Silicone Mold*: Liquid silicone rubber supported by a rigid plaster mother mold is applied over each section.
     - *Step 4: Hollow Wax Pattern & Refractory Core*:
       1. Molten wax is brushed/slushed inside the rubber mold to produce a uniform hollow wax pattern (5–8 mm thick).
       2. The hollow interior is filled with a refractory core mix, and bronze chaplets (pins) are inserted through the wax into the core to anchor it firmly.
     - *Step 5: Spruing & Gating*: Wax gates, risers, runners, and air vents are welded onto the wax pattern to form a pouring tree.
     - *Step 6: Ceramic Investment (Shell Building)*: The assembly is repeatedly dipped in refractory colloidal silica slurry and coated with zircon stucco sand (6–10 coats), building a strong ceramic shell.
     - *Step 7: Dewaxing & Firing*:
       1. Inverted in an autoclave ($140^\circ\text{C}-175^\circ\text{C}$); the wax melts out completely (lost wax).
       2. The shell is fired at $900^\circ\text{C}-1050^\circ\text{C}$ in a furnace to sinter the ceramic and burn out wax residue.
     - *Step 8: Pouring*: Molten bronze (Cu-Sn-Zn-Pb at $1150^\circ\text{C}-1200^\circ\text{C}$) is poured into the preheated ceramic mold.
     - *Step 9: Knockout & De-coring*: After cooling, the ceramic shell is broken away with pneumatic hammers, and internal core material is excavated.
     - *Step 10: Assembly, Welding & Chasing*: Bronze sections are aligned and TIG-welded together using matching bronze filler rod. Seams are ground and chased with hand chisels to replicate original textures.
     - *Step 11: Chemical Patination & Sealing*: The bronze is torch-heated and treated with chemical salts (e.g., cupric nitrate, liver of sulfur) to develop an antique brown or green patina; sealed with protective wax/lacquer for outdoor weather resistance.

### 57. Describe the differences between expendable and permanent molds in metal casting.
- **題目翻譯**：請詳細描述金屬鑄造中「消耗模（Expendable Molds）」與「永久模（Permanent Molds）」之間的差異。
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 11` Slide 3, 4, 23
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答 - 分項結構化對比)**:
  #### 1. Mold Reusability:
  - **Expendable Molds**: **Destroyed / broken after every single casting cycle** (one-time use only); a new mold must be made for each part.
  - **Permanent Molds**: **Reused repeatedly for thousands to hundreds of thousands of shots** without destroying the mold.

  #### 2. Mold Materials:
  - **Expendable Molds**: Silica sand, plaster, ceramic slurries, bonded with clay, resins, or silicates.
  - **Permanent Molds**: High-alloy tool steels (H13), cast irons, refractory metals, or graphite.

  #### 3. Thermal Conductivity & Cooling Rate:
  - **Expendable Molds**: **Low thermal conductivity (insulative)**; molten metal cools slowly, leading to longer cycle times and coarser grains.
  - **Permanent Molds**: **High thermal conductivity**; rapid heat extraction produces fast chilling, fine grains, and superior strength.

  #### 4. Part Complexity & Undercuts:
  - **Expendable Molds**: **Virtually unlimited**; complex internal passages and deep undercuts are easily formed using collapsible cores.
  - **Permanent Molds**: **Restricted by draft angles and parting lines**; undercuts require expensive retractable mechanical slides.

  #### 5. Tooling & Equipment Cost:
  - **Expendable Molds**: **Low initial tooling costs**; short lead times for mold preparation.
  - **Permanent Molds**: **Extremely high die fabrication and machine costs**; high initial capital investment.

  #### 6. Production Volume Suitability:
  - **Expendable Molds**: Ideal for **prototypes, custom one-off parts, small batches, or ultra-large castings**.
  - **Permanent Molds**: Economical only for **large-scale mass production** to amortize high tooling costs.

  #### 7. Labor Cost per Casting:
  - **Expendable Molds**: High (labor-intensive mold making, pouring, and shakeout).
  - **Permanent Molds**: Low (highly automated cycle; low manual intervention).

  #### 8. Dimensional Precision & Surface Finish:
  - **Expendable Molds**: Moderate to low precision ($\pm 1.0-3.0\text{ mm}$); rougher surface ($Ra \approx 6-25\;\mu\text{m}$).
  - **Permanent Molds**: High precision ($\pm 0.1-0.3\text{ mm}$); smooth surface finish ($Ra \approx 1.5-3.5\;\mu\text{m}$).
- **中文觀念解析**：
  消耗模每件破壞一次（砂模、陶瓷模，適合小批量複雜件與巨型件，模具費低）；永久模反覆使用數萬次（耐熱鋼模，適合大批量，模具昂貴但精度高、冷卻快晶粒細、自動化成本低）。

### 58. Explain the investment casting process.
- **題目翻譯**：請詳細解釋「包模鑄造法 / 脫蠟鑄造法（Investment casting / Lost-wax process）」的完整工藝流程。
- **對應簡報**：`MFG_chap 11` Slide 17, 18, 19, 20
- **Model Answer for Exam (英文標準作答)**:
  **Investment Casting (also known as the Lost-Wax Process)** is a premier precision near-net-shape manufacturing process. Its complete step-by-step engineering procedure is:
  1. **Wax Pattern Production**: Molten wax or thermoplastic is injected under pressure into a precision metal master die, solidifying into an accurate, disposable replica pattern.
  2. **Tree Assembly (Spruing)**: Multiple individual wax patterns are attached via wax runners to a central wax sprue, creating a multi-part "wax tree" (cluster) to cast dozens of parts simultaneously.
  3. **Slurry Dipping (Investment)**: The wax cluster is dipped into a liquid ceramic refractory slurry consisting of fine zircon/silica powders mixed with colloidal silica binder.
  4. **Stuccoing and Drying**: While the slurry is wet, coarse refractory ceramic stucco sand is showered over the cluster in a fluidized bed. The layer is dried under controlled temperature and humidity.
     - *Repeated Cycles*: Steps 3 and 4 are repeated **6 to 8 times** to build up a thick, rigid, monolithic ceramic shell.
  5. **Dewaxing (Lost-Wax)**: The ceramic mold is inverted in an autoclave ($140^\circ\text{C}-175^\circ\text{C}$ at 7 bar). The wax melts rapidly and drains out completely within minutes, leaving clean, hollow ceramic cavities without cracking the shell.
  6. **Burnout and Sintering (Firing)**: The shell is fired in a kiln at **$650^\circ\text{C}-1050^\circ\text{C}$** to burn out all residual wax traces, remove crystalline water, and sinter the ceramic particles into high hot strength.
  7. **Pouring into Preheated Shell**: Molten metal is poured directly into the hot ceramic shell ($700^\circ\text{C}-900^\circ\text{C}$). The hot mold prevents premature metal chilling, enabling complete filling of ultra-thin walls ($0.5\text{ mm}$) and fine blade tips.
  8. **Cooling and Shell Knockout**: After cooling, the brittle ceramic shell is broken away using pneumatic hammers, high-pressure water blasting, or chemical leaching.
  9. **Cut-off, Finishing & Inspection**: Cast parts are severed from the central sprue with abrasive cut-off wheels; gates are ground flush; components undergo heat treatment, polishing, and NDT inspection (X-ray, fluorescent penetrant).
